CASE STUDY 04 · CSR Advisory

Impact Assessment Across a Multi-Thematic CSR Portfolio

Programme Profile

A diversified CSR portfolio comprising programmes across rural development, water security, agriculture and livelihoods, education, environmental restoration, healthcare and other community-focused interventions.

The broader impact-assessment portfolio covered 24 CSR projects across multiple states in India, providing exposure to programmes with substantially different beneficiary groups, intervention models and intended outcomes.

Challenge

The portfolio required a consistent and evidence-based assessment of programmes operating across different social and environmental themes.

The assessments needed to go beyond reviewing programme activities and outputs and establish a structured understanding of programme relevance, effectiveness, efficiency, impact and sustainability, while appropriately capturing the perspectives of beneficiaries and other stakeholders.

My Professional Contribution

  • Contributed to impact assessments across 24 CSR projects.
  • Supported development of project-specific beneficiary questionnaires.
  • Contributed to determination of appropriate beneficiary sampling requirements.
  • Participated in beneficiary surveys and field assessments.
  • Supported Focus Group Discussions, Key Informant Interviews and stakeholder consultations.
  • Contributed to quantitative and qualitative data analysis.
  • Contributed to programme evaluation using the OECD-DAC criteria.
  • Supported triangulation of beneficiary responses, programme documentation and field-level evidence.
  • Contributed to development of project-level findings and recommendations.
  • Supported preparation and refinement of impact-assessment reports.

Outcome

Supported evidence-based impact assessment across 24 CSR projects, combining quantitative beneficiary research with qualitative stakeholder engagement and field-level assessment.

For programmes with sufficiently large beneficiary populations, the sampling design was developed using a 95% confidence level and 5% margin of error, providing a statistically informed basis for beneficiary surveys.

The assessments combined beneficiary surveys with FGDs, KIIs, IDIs, field observations and programme documentation, enabling findings to be triangulated across multiple evidence sources.

The resulting assessments provided structured insights into programme performance against the OECD-DAC criteria, along with recommendations to support future programme strengthening, implementation and monitoring.
